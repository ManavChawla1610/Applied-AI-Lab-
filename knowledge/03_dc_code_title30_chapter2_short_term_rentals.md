# DC Code, Title 30, Chapter 2: Short-Term Rental Regulation

## § 30-201.01 Definitions
- "Host": a natural person who uses a booking service to provide a short-term rental to a transient guest.
- "Primary residence": property eligible for the homestead deduction under § 47-850.
- "Short-term rental": paid lodging for transient guests with the host present, unless it is a vacation rental. It is not a hotel, inn, motel, boarding house, rooming house or bed and breakfast, and operates within a portion of the host's residential property unless it is a vacation rental.
- "Vacation rental": a short-term rental where the guest has exclusive use of the property and the host is not present; subject to additional restrictions including § 30-201.06(e).

## § 30-201.02 Restrictions
- A basic business license with a "Short-Term Rental" endorsement is required (a vacation rental needs a "Short-Term Rental: Vacation Rental" endorsement).
- Liability insurance is required and may be provided by a booking service. The amount hosts must show is **at least $250,000**, per DLCP's licensing requirements (file 01; confirmed by the project team).
- Hosts must give each guest a 24-hour accessible phone number for emergencies.
- The property must be the host's primary residence (§ 30-201.02(d)).
- A host may offer multiple short-term rentals at one property (e.g., a bedroom and an in-law suite), subject to occupancy limits.
- A vacation rental guest is not a resident's guest for annual visitor parking permits.

## § 30-201.03 License application
The host must show compliance, provide proof that a condominium, cooperative or homeowner association permits short-term rentals (if applicable), describe the rentals, and pay the fee. The two endorsement types cost the same.

## § 30-201.04 Issuance
Endorsements go only to hosts, one per host, valid for two years. The endorsement applies to the property; more than one rental at the property does not need separate endorsements. No inspection is required before issuance.

## § 30-201.05 Sanctions
The Mayor sets suspension, revocation and other licensing sanctions by rulemaking.

## § 30-201.06 Requirements for short-term rentals
- Post the license inside the rental and provide the emergency number.
- Maintain a working smoke detector outside the sleeping area and on all habitable floors, and a working carbon monoxide detector on all habitable floors.
- Provide unobstructed egress.
- Clean between guests, including changing bed linens and towels.
- Keep booking records for two years and pay applicable transient lodging taxes.
- **(e)** A short-term rental may operate as a vacation rental for no more than 90 nights cumulatively in any calendar year unless the host has an exemption under subsection (f) (certain work or serious-health situations).
- Occupancy: no more than 8 guests or 2 per bedroom, whichever is greater.
- A host may not list on a booking service that does not allow the license endorsement number to be displayed in the listing.

## § 30-201.07 Guest rights
Transient guests are not treated as tenants under the cited housing laws.

## § 30-201.08 Booking services
Booking services must let hosts display the endorsement number, must not book suspended or revoked rentals, must not book a vacation rental beyond 90 nights without a DLCP exemption notice, must file monthly transaction reports, keep records for two years, obtain host consent to disclosure, and collect taxes.

## § 30-201.09 Enforcement
DLCP monitors and investigates violations, can issue penalties or refer cases to the Attorney General. The Office of the Chief Financial Officer handles certain record and tax provisions. A listing that suggests exclusive use of the whole residence is prima facie evidence of a vacation rental.

## § 30-201.10 Penalties
- Hosts who violate § 30-201.02 or § 30-201.06: civil penalty of $500 for a first violation, $2,000 for a second, and $6,000 plus revocation of the license endorsement for a third.
- Booking services that violate § 30-201.08: $1,000 per violating booking transaction.
- The Mayor may adjust these amounts by rulemaking.
- See 13_dc_penalties_interest_and_liens.md for interest and liens on unpaid fines.

## § 30-201.11 Rules
The Mayor may issue implementing rules (14 DCMR Chapter 99).

## Subchapter II, § 30-210.01 Funding
Sets out how revenue losses from Subchapter I are offset and requires the Chief Financial Officer to certify funding status in revenue estimates.
