# Vacation Rental Exemption from DC's 90-Night Limit

## The rule
A short-term rental may operate as a vacation rental (host away) for no more than 90 nights cumulatively per calendar year unless the host has an exemption under 14 DCMR § 9904.

## Who qualifies (14 DCMR § 9904.2)
- **Employment:** the host, or the host's spouse or domestic partner, is required by an employer to work outside DC for more than 90 cumulative days in a calendar year.
- **Medical:** the host must leave DC for more than 90 cumulative days in a calendar year to receive treatment for a serious health condition, or to care for a family member receiving such treatment ("serious health condition" as defined in DC Official Code § 32-541.01(20)).

## Before applying
The host must already hold an active Vacation Rental license. DLCP's web page says online applications are reviewed within 2–3 business days (apply from the license on the boss.dc.gov dashboard → Actions → "Apply for Exemption").

## What to submit
1. The completed application (all fields required): calendar year; host name, primary residence address, phone, email; dates the host will live outside DC and the address there; spouse/partner or family member details if applicable; employer name/address and dates (employment) or medical provider, treatment location and dates (medical).
2. **Proof of travel:** e.g., airline tickets, hotel reservations outside DC, a lease outside DC, or other documents showing the host will live outside DC.
3. **Notarized affidavit:** Part A from the employer (work location, dates, scope of work) or Part B from the health care provider (patient, treatment location, dates).
4. **Host certification:** the host certifies ownership of the primary residence, the address and dates outside DC, and an explanation of the need for travel, signed and dated.

Send the application and attachments to shorttermrentals@dc.gov (or upload through the online portal).

## After approval
The host must notify the DC Short-Term Rental Program within 10 days of returning to DC.
