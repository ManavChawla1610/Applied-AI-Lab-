# Federal Income Tax on Renting Your Home (IRS Topic 415)

## Rental income and expenses
- Rental income from a dwelling unit (house, apartment) can be reduced by deductible expenses such as mortgage interest, real estate taxes, casualty losses, maintenance, utilities, insurance and depreciation.
- Report on Form 1040 or 1040-SR and Schedule E (Form 1040).
- If you rent to make a profit and do not use the unit as a residence, deductible expenses can exceed gross rental income; losses are generally limited by the at-risk and passive activity loss rules.

## Rented fewer than 15 days (the "14-day rule")
If you use a unit as a residence and rent it for fewer than 15 days in the year, you do not report the rental income and do not deduct rental expenses.

## What counts as a personal-use day
A day is personal use if the unit is used by: you or another owner (unless rented to them as their main home at a fair rental price under a shared equity agreement); a family member of an owner (unless it is their main home and they pay a fair rental price); anyone under an arrangement that lets you use another dwelling; or anyone paying less than a fair rental price.

## When the unit counts as your residence
You use it as a residence if personal use during the year exceeds the greater of **14 days** or **10% of the days it is rented at a fair rental price**. You can have more than one residence in a year.

## Mixed rental and personal use
- Divide total expenses between rental and personal use based on the days used for each.
- Rental expenses cannot exceed gross rental income minus the rental portion of mortgage interest, real estate taxes, casualty losses and direct rental expenses (e.g., advertising); excess may carry forward to the next year.
- If you itemize on Schedule A, you may deduct the personal portion of mortgage interest and property taxes, plus casualty losses from federally declared disasters.

## Other
Rental income may be subject to the net investment income tax (Topic 559).
