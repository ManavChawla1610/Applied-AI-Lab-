# Penalties, Interest and Liens for DC Short-Term Rental Violations

## The penalties (D.C. Code § 30-201.10)
- **Hosts** found to have violated § 30-201.02 (restrictions, such as licensing, primary residence, insurance, emergency contact) or § 30-201.06 (operating requirements, such as posting the license, detectors, egress, cleaning, records, the 90-night vacation rental cap, occupancy):
  - **$500** for a first violation
  - **$2,000** for a second violation
  - **$6,000** for a third violation, **plus revocation** of the short-term rental license endorsement
- **Booking services** that violate § 30-201.08: **$1,000 per booking transaction** made in violation.
- The Mayor may adjust these penalties by rulemaking.
- Source note: D.C. Law 22-307, § 110 (Apr. 25, 2019), 66 DCR 898.

## Who enforces (D.C. Code § 30-201.09)
DLCP maintains records on licensed short-term rentals, monitors listings and compliance, notifies the booking service and host when it finds a violation, and can issue penalties or refer cases to the Attorney General. If a listing claims or suggests the guest will have exclusive use of the host's entire residence, that is prima facie evidence the rental is a vacation rental, whatever license was issued.

## Interest and liens on unpaid fines
§ 30-201.10 itself says nothing about interest, late fees or liens. Those come from DC's general laws on civil fines, which the DC Department of Buildings explains on its enforcement page:
- **Legal basis:** D.C. Official Code § 2-1802.03(i) and § 47-1205 require fines and special assessments to be paid in full with interest from the date levied.
- **Interest rate:** **1.5% per month** (or portion of a month) that a fine remains unpaid.
- **When interest starts:** 30 calendar days after the fine is levied.
- **Liens:** when fines or special assessments are unpaid, a **lien is placed on the property** for the amount due.
- **Collections:** fines unpaid 180 days are treated as delinquent and transferred to the District's Central Collection Unit.
- **Tax sale:** unpaid special assessments may be sent to the Office of Tax and Revenue for inclusion in the annual real property tax sale.

Separately, unpaid DC debts over $1,000 make a host ineligible for the Certificate of Clean Hands that the license requires (file 01), so an unpaid fine can also block licensing or renewal.

## How to answer users
State the § 30-201.10 penalty amounts directly. For interest and liens, say that DC law requires unpaid civil fines to be paid with interest, that DC's published practice is 1.5% per month starting 30 days after the fine and a lien on the property, and that the exact treatment of a specific short-term rental fine should be confirmed with DLCP at (202) 221-8550. Do not give legal advice.
