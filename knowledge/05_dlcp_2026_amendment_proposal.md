# Proposed 2026 Changes to DC Short-Term Rental Law (PROPOSED, NOT IN EFFECT)

## Announcement
On Friday, March 13, 2026, Mayor Muriel Bowser and DLCP announced the Short-Term Rental Regulation Amendment Act of 2026, intended to clarify DC's short-term rental laws and expand earning opportunities for residents. As of retrieval, check DLCP or the DC Council for whether it has passed.

## Proposed provisions
- **Renters could host:** renters could operate a short-term rental at their primary residence, unless the unit is in the Rent Stabilization Program or the lease restricts it.
- **One license type:** removes the distinction between Short-Term Rental and Vacation Rental licenses.
- **Special event license:** a new license to rent during special events and holidays designated by the Mayor, without the host present.
- **Primary residence definition:** drops the Homestead Deduction reference; hosts must live at the property they rent.
- **Second property:** a license for a second DC property that does not need to be owner-occupied; if unoccupied, it could be rented no more than 90 cumulative nights per year. A special event license would also be available for it.

## How to answer users
Always say these are proposals, not current law, and that today's rules (primary residence with Homestead Deduction, 90-night cap for vacation rentals) still apply until DC enacts a change.
